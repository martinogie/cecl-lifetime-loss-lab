# Phase 1, step 6: our sample vs Freddie's published summary statistics

- **Status:** done for the sample, unweighted (15 November 2026). A weighted comparison follows for the
  full-data vintages (ADR-001, ADR-007).
- **Data:** Freddie Mac Single-Family Loan-Level Dataset, Release 48 (originations and performance to
  30 June 2026). Our side: the 28 sample years (1,375,000 loans). Freddie's side: two Clarity exports
  (Dataset Type = All, HARP included), downloaded by Martins. Freddie's own figures are not
  republished here, only our figures and the differences.
- **Source:** Freddie Mac Clarity, Standard Dataset Summary Statistics, viewed 15 November 2026,
  Dataset Type = All, UPB-weighted. Clarity describes its figures as **for reference only**; the
  loan-level dataset is the official source. Educational model, not an audited reserve.
- **Tables:** [step6_freddie_tables.md](step6_freddie_tables.md) (our figures and the differences),
  regenerated by `python -m scripts.compare_clarity_summary`.

## How the comparison works

Freddie's figures cover every loan; ours come from a sample, so each of ours has sampling error. For
every figure we compute a standard error and a z-score (how many standard errors ours is from
Freddie's): within ±2 "agrees", 2 to 3 "borderline", beyond 3 "differs". With about 420 comparisons,
roughly 20 borderline results are expected by chance. Freddie's figures are weighted by original
balance (UPB), so ours are too; loan-count versions are shown alongside.

"Freddie's way" uses Freddie's own definitions with no stop-the-clock: **Default** = loan-ending codes
02, 03, 09 and 15 (checked: Freddie's Default equals its REO Disposition + Foreclosure Alternative +
NPL columns, and Active + Prepay + Default + RPL + Defect = 100%); **Ever D90+** = ever reported 3+
months late; **Ever forbearance** = ever in a forbearance plan; **Ever modified** = ever modified.
Vintage is the origination year in the loan ID (L-018).

## Findings

**1. The sample is drawn equally by quarter, and that explains the biggest differences.** Every year
has exactly 12,500 loans in each origination quarter, although Freddie's user guide describes "a
simple random sample of 50,000 loans" per year. Real lending volume swings between quarters, so a
yearly average over the sample gives low-volume quarters too much weight compared with Freddie's
population figures.

The effect is clearest in the **interest rate**: ours is above Freddie's in 27 of 28 years, and the
size of the gap tracks how far rates moved within the year (**correlation +0.91** across the 28
vintages). Where rates barely moved, the gap is tiny (2005: rates ranged 0.37 points across the
quarters; gap +0.005 points). Where they moved most, it is largest (2022: rates rose from 3.55% in Q1
to 6.36% in Q4 while volume fell; gap +0.35 points). Other measures move less within a year, so the
effect is smaller and often within sampling error. No weights are applied (ADR-007, L-019): models
and the reserve use the sample as it is. The weighted comparison comes when the full-data vintages are
loaded and each quarter's true loan count is known.

**2. Freddie's "DTI > 45%" and "FICO < 680%" divide by all loans,** counting "Not Available" as not
over 45 / not under 680. This matters in 2010–2013, when 23–30% of loans (mostly HARP refinances)
have no DTI. With that denominator, DTI > 45 agrees in 22 of 28 years (12 before the fix).

**3. Agreement, unweighted:** credit-profile measures agree in 14–22 of 28 years each (OLTV 14, CLTV
14, FICO 16, DTI 18, investor 22, cash-out 22, DTI > 45 22, FICO < 680 20), with rate the exception
(finding 1). Performance, Freddie's way, agrees in 18–25 of 28 years (Active 19, Prepay 18, Default 19,
Ever D90+ 22, Ever forbearance 22, Ever modified 25). The remaining differences are consistent with the
quarter mix in finding 1; none points to a loading or definition error.

**4. Our ADR-002 default vs Freddie's way (Ever D90+).** After L-020 (below), the two agree almost
exactly for 1999–2008 (ADR-002 lower by at most 0.16 points). The remaining gap comes from
**stop-the-clock forbearance** (ADR-002 D):

| Vintages | ADR-002 minus our Ever D90+ | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1999–2008 | −0.16 to 0.00 points | almost no flagged forbearance |
| 2009–2014 | −1.88 to −0.44 points | loans still outstanding during the 2017 hurricanes and COVID |
| **2015–2019** | **−4.51 to −2.63 points** | the loans outstanding when COVID forbearance hit in 2020–2021 |
| 2020–2021 | −1.52 to −0.70 points | originated during or after the pause; less exposure |
| 2022–2026 | −0.96 to 0.00 points | later disasters and remaining pauses |

The gap is biggest for the 2015–2019 vintages, not 2020–2021: COVID forbearance applied to loans
*outstanding* in 2020–2021, mostly originated in 2015–2019. Freddie's own Ever forbearance figure is
highest for those vintages, several times its level for 2020 loans, and the stop-the-clock effect
follows it. Our own sample confirms it (tables, "COVID forbearance in our sample"): forbearance
loan-months jump in April 2020 and peak in June 2020 (4.7% of active loan-months), and 62% of all
2020–2021 forbearance loan-months fall on the 2015–2019 vintages (3.8–6.6% of each vintage's loans),
against 1.9% of 2020 loans and 0.2% of 2021 loans. So ADR-001's COVID full-data vintage is 2019
(optionally 2017), not 2020 or 2021. Smaller effects in the other direction: short sales, REO acquisitions and the 60-day
modification rule (ADR-002 A, C) catch about 165 defaults before a 90+ report. *First default only*
creates no gap: both measures count a loan once.

**L-020 (decided 15 November 2026):** a loan 90+ days late (stopped clock) in the same month it pays
off (code 01) now counts as a default that lost nothing. Effect: 1,440 loans moved from prepaid to
defaulted, mostly 1999–2008; default rates rose by at most 0.4 points (1999: 3.04% to 3.44%) and are
unchanged from 2019. 73 loans that paid off while 90+ days behind only during forbearance stay prepaid,
and 18 loans 90+ days late in their defect month (code 96) stay censored.

**5. One loan's vintage differs from its file** (L-018): it sits in the 2008 sample file but its loan
ID says 2009, so our vintages show 49,999 (2008) and 50,001 (2009). Too small to affect any comparison
here.
